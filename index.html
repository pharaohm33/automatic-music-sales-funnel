Fan-funnel system · borrowed promo to owned list

The Automatic Music Sales Funnel

One system, not five separate ones: paid promo drives strangers into your music, your music drives them onto your Beehiiv list, and your list sells merch, shows, and giveaways on autopilot for the next year.

By MixedByEmmanuel.com

In short: you drive free and/or paid traffic — influencer marketing plus your own efforts — to a YouTube playlist. That playlist trades something the audience actually wants (a giveaway, unreleased content, whatever the hook is) for an email signup. From there, you send that list entertaining emails all year — new videos, behind-the-scenes content, updates — and in the footer of those emails, every week or multiple times a week, you give people a way to buy merch, show tickets, and deluxe versions of releases.
The mistake you named: running promo, a playlist, and an email list as three disconnected projects. They only pay off as one pipe — promo buys attention, YouTube proves you're worth following, the popup converts that attention into an owned channel, and Beehiiv's automation turns a single visit into fifty-two weeks of revenue touches.
01 · The mechanism

How one listener becomes a year of touchpoints

Six linked stages. The promo buy only has to work once per campaign — after that, every video you publish keeps feeding the same list for free. YouTube is the platform to anchor this on: subscribers actually see new uploads, where TikTok hands that decision to the algorithm — so the list-building CTA in your description reaches the same people reliably, upload after upload, instead of depending on whether a given post gets pushed to feeds.

02 · Influencer outreach

Getting stage 01 for as little as possible

Micro-influencers (5K–75K followers, genre- or scene-adjacent) convert better per dollar than one big placement — and they'll negotiate. The goal is the same reach for less money, split across more accounts so no single post carries the whole campaign.

CHECK 1

Engagement over follower count

Look for a 3–8% engagement rate (likes+comments ÷ followers on recent posts). A 12K-follower page averaging 900 real comments beats a 100K page averaging 200.

CHECK 2

Real comments, real audience

Skim the last 10 posts' comments. Genuine reactions and replies from the creator are good signs; rows of emoji-only comments from unrelated accounts mean bought followers.

CHECK 3

Genre or scene overlap

Their audience should already listen to something adjacent to you — a curator, a similar artist's fan page, a local scene account. Overlap matters more than raw size.

Script 1 · Shoutout for shoutoutuse with pages close to your own size
Hey [name] — love what you're doing with [their page/account], especially [specific recent post]. I make [genre] music and think our audiences overlap a lot. Want to do a shoutout swap this week — I post you to my list/page, you post my new video to yours? No money involved, just trading reach. Happy to go first if that helps.
Naming a specific post proves you're not mass-DMing — it's the single biggest lever on reply rate.
Script 2 · Cold paid askuse with pages bigger than yours, no existing relationship
Hey [name], I'm an independent [genre] artist and I think your audience would genuinely like my new video — [one-line reason, tied to something they've posted before]. Do you do paid story shares or feature posts? I've got a small budget and would love to hear your rate, including whether you'd do a run of a few posts over the month instead of just one.
Asking their rate first (instead of offering a number) keeps you from anchoring high — and "a few posts over the month" is the opener for Script 3.
Script 3 · Negotiating volumeuse once they've quoted a single-post price
That works for me — instead of one post, what would you charge for about 4 story shares a week for the month, same clip or link, swapped out when I've got something new? It's less work for you than a fresh post each time, and steady reach works better for me than one spike.
4×/week keeps you showing up in their audience's stories constantly — the whole point isn't one big hit, it's repeated exposure so someone who scrolls past it three times finally clicks the fourth. Framing it as "less work for you" (a re-share, not a new post) is why $10–$100 for a month of that cadence is realistic on smaller pages — you're buying repetition, not production.

03 · Stage by stage

What to actually set up

01
Buy cheap reach from a bigger page
Pay or trade for a shoutout/feature from an account with an audience adjacent to yours — not necessarily huge, just bigger and relevant. The only job of this post is to send people to one piece of your music.
cost: low, one-timechannel: IG/TikTok page, playlist curator, blog
02
Land them on a YouTube playlist, not one video
Send the promo link to a playlist — your music videos plus vlogs, behind-the-scenes, anything you've got — instead of a single clip. A single video only gives you one shot at the signup CTA; a playlist autoplays into the next video and the next, and every single one carries the same call-to-action. More videos watched means more chances they see it and finally click, instead of relying on one video to convert on its own. The bar to clear here is low: any playlist where every video's description links to your Beehiiv signup makes the whole system work — it doesn't need to be more complicated than that.
tool: YouTube playlist (music videos + vlogs)every video description carries the same CTA
03
Capture with a giveaway, not a newsletter pitch
"Sign up for a chance to win [merch / a signed print / a shoutout]" converts far better than "join my newsletter." Put this as a popup on your linked landing page — but don't depend on it. A popup can get blocked, skipped, or just not show on mobile, so the CTA has to lead every video description, not sit buried under lyrics/credits where most viewers never scroll: first line, above the fold, before anything else. Layer the popup on top if you have one; the description line is the real backbone of this step either way. Same rule everywhere you place this link — description, popup, bio, email footer: make it an actual tappable link, not typed-out text someone has to copy and paste. A dead URL is a lost signup at every single one of these touchpoints, not just the influencer stories covered below.
tool: Beehiiv signup form or a link-in-bio pageCTA is line one of every description
04
Land them in Beehiiv, tagged by source
Every signup enters Beehiiv with a tag for where they came from (this campaign vs. organic vs. a specific video). That tag is what lets you measure whether a given promo buy was worth it — and lets you re-target "giveaway entrants" separately from long-time fans later.

Source tagging like this works on the free plan. Real list segmentation — grouping subscribers by city, by what they signed up for, by anything you define, and sending a given email only to that group — is a paid-plan feature. Example: fans in Austin get an email when you announce an Austin show; fans everywhere else don't. Or fans who entered the merch giveaway get merch-focused emails while fans who entered the meet-and-greet giveaway get a different track. Free plan sends the same email to your whole list every time; paid segmentation is what lets the same weekly slot feel personal instead of generic. Walkthrough: Beehiiv list segmentation tutorial.

05 · The weekly engine

A five-week rotation, repeated all year

Weighted toward low-cost, frequent giveaways — autographs and shoutouts cost you almost nothing and keep open rates high between the rarer paid-entry drops.

Set a standing shop footer in Beehiiv itself, not a link pasted into each email. Beehiiv has a real Footer setting (Settings → Design → Footer) that attaches automatically to every send — welcome series, weekly broadcast, one-off automations, all of it — so you build it once and never touch it again. Put merch and anything else purchasable there: a quiet "Shop merch →" and, if you sell more than one thing, a second line for the other paid offer (tickets, paid-entry giveaways, whatever's live). Same spot every time means it never competes with that week's actual topic and never reads as a pitch — it's just always there to click if someone's already in a buying mood. Walkthrough: Beehiiv footer tutorial.
Every new video: two things, every time. Add it to the playlist, and get it in front of the list — either automatically (a Beehiiv automation triggered off your YouTube channel's RSS feed will draft an email the moment you publish) or manually by dropping it straight into the next Week A slot below. Skipping either half breaks the loop: no playlist add means influencer links and old video descriptions stop compounding toward it; no email means the list never hears you released anything.

06 · Setup order

Build it in this order, once

  • Create the Beehiiv publication and one signup form with a source-tag field. ~30 min.
  • Write the 5-day welcome automation in Beehiiv (Day 0/1/3/7/11 above). ~2 hrs.
  • Write 6 rotating templates for Weeks A–E plus the quarterly paid giveaway, and schedule the recurring weekly broadcast. ~3 hrs, done once.
  • Set Beehiiv's Footer (Settings → Design → Footer) with a shop-merch link, not a per-email paste — it then applies to every send automatically, welcome series included. ~15 min, done once.
  • Check every link is actually clickable — description, popup, bio, email footer — not typed-out text someone has to copy. A dead link loses the signup right there.
  • Put a QR code on the physical merch — tag, label, or packaging — linking to the Beehiiv signup, plus one to buy more merch. Takes the same loop offline.
  • Build the playlist — every music video plus vlogs/BTS you have, ordered so it autoplays from strongest to newest. ~30 min.
  • Set the standing rule: every new video gets added to the playlist AND sent to the list — either an RSS-triggered Beehiiv automation or a manual drop into the next Week A slot. Ongoing, not a one-time setup step.
  • Put the signup CTA as line one of every video's description — above lyrics, credits, or anything else, since most viewers never scroll past the fold. This is the placement that has to work no matter what a popup does. ~1 hr.
  • Vet and DM 6–10 micro-influencer pages using the scripts above, negotiating for ~4 story shares a week for the month, not a single post. ~2–3 hrs.
  • Confirm each influencer's story uses a link sticker pointed at your playlist, with a per-creator tracked link.
  • Watch signups land tagged in Beehiiv, confirm the welcome automation fires, then leave it running.
  • (iOS) Try [untitled] for selling bonus/unreleased editions to your list at your own price, sent as its own email whenever ready — no fixed schedule needed.
